# Start Applying

*Section 4: Agents · Lesson 24 of 32 · about 3 hours, then 1 hour a week*

## Introduction

> **Hireable doesn't mean finished. It means two repos you can explain.**

You now have projects you can show and explain. That's enough to start applying for jobs, so don't wait until the end of the path.

Job hunting gets its own weekly time block, separate from your study sessions, and it isn't tracked on the progress bar. Rejections say nothing about your progress here.

**After this, you can** tell three true stories about your work in 3 minutes.

**The skill that lasts:** explaining trade-offs to people who don't build.

## Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Know what AI engineering interviews look like.
- Tell clear stories about your projects.
- Set up a weekly job application routine.

## Assignment

1. Read the interview and take-home sections of the [AI Engineering Field Guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide). Try one take-home task with a time limit.
2. Add Poster Reader and Ask MDN to your resume ([resume guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/)). Link the public repos and lead with the numbers from your READMEs.
3. Prepare three short stories from your log: a hard bug, a design choice and a mistake you fixed ([behavioral interview guide](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/behavioral-interview/)).
4. Practise explaining how Ask MDN works, out loud, in 3 minutes.
5. Put a separate 1-hour block in your calendar each week to apply to 2 or 3 jobs, until you finish Make It Sturdy. After that, give it more time.

### Recall · 10 min

6. Close this page. In your log, answer from memory, then check:
   - Which project numbers belong on your resume? ([where to check](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/))
   - How does `interrupt()` keep `save_note` safe? ([where to check](23-project-give-it-tools.md))
7. Write one more line: how will this lesson help your Campus Assistant?
