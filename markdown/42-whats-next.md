# What's Next

*Section 7: Conclusion · Lesson 42 of 42 · about 2 weeks (spare)*

## Introduction

*You built an assistant and tested it with real students, or in a clearly labelled rehearsal. These two weeks check that your skills work on material you've never seen, and leave room to repair one weak spot.*

> **The final test is a new problem, small enough to finish, different enough to make you think.**

You have reached the end of the guided path. The question now is: can you apply the ideas when the data and requirements change? The Electives that follow are optional, not more required work.

Use this time to repair gaps and try one small independent task. You've finished the Campus Assistant. This check shows whether your skills carry over to material you've never seen.

**After this, you can** test whether your skills transfer to a new dataset and choose your next practice task.

**The skill that lasts:** scoping, and saying no to features.

## Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Show that your skills work on public material you haven't used before.
- Find a weak skill and pick one exercise to fix it.
- Choose your next project, or a deeper track for the role you want.

## Assignment

1. **Days 1–2:** Finish any required project checks you haven't done yet. Then pick one public source you haven't used, such as a small museum visitor FAQ or an open-source project's installation guide. Keep it to 5–10 pages, and record its source and reuse terms.
2. Before you write any code, write a short brief, 6 expected question and answer cases (2 of them unanswerable from the source), and a cost limit. Stop when the brief and cases are committed.
3. **Days 3–6:** Spend 3–6 hours adapting your existing app. First choose the simplest design that works, and explain whether you need retrieval or an agent. You may use documentation and your own code. Keep AI in explain-and-review mode: it must not design or write the solution. You don't need a new framework. Stop when your 6 cases run.
4. **Days 7–9:** Demo 3 questions you haven't tried before, and one failure.
5. Ask a friend to plant one bug in your copy. Or plant one yourself a week earlier and forget the details. Find the bug from its symptoms: trace the full request and show your evidence.
6. Then make one small change to the requirements without an AI-written patch. Ask a friend to choose the change if you can. If not, add a new document format or a restriction you didn't build for.
7. If you can't finish on your own, go back to the relevant lesson and try again. This is a check, not a speed contest. Stop when the bug is found and the change is committed.
8. **Days 10–12:** Write a one-page evidence review: what you built, where it failed, which skill still needs practice, and what you would change.
9. Decide the Campus Assistant's next step from your pilot evidence: widen the pilot, add a second university, or pause and fix one weakness first.
10. Add a portfolio evidence table: requirement → code → test → result → limitation. Ask someone to review one requirement, or record a ten-minute walkthrough yourself.
11. Keep your weekly application routine. Start applying to applied AI and backend roles, and use interview feedback to choose your next repair task.
12. Choose deeper study only for a specific goal: [ML Zoomcamp](https://github.com/DataTalksClub/machine-learning-zoomcamp) for broader ML modelling, [LLMs from Scratch](https://github.com/rasbt/LLMs-from-scratch) for model internals, or serving and infrastructure work for an AI platform role. This path covers applied LLM applications. It does not claim mastery of every AI specialty, or protection from automation.

- [ ] Your small new-domain app meets the brief and recorded checks, or your evidence review clearly identifies what needs repair.
