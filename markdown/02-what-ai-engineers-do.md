# What AI Engineers Do

*Section 1: Introduction · Lesson 2 of 41 · about 1.5 hours*

## Introduction

> **Most of an AI engineer's job is normal software work. The new part is a model that sounds sure even when it's wrong.**

This path teaches applied AI engineering: building useful software around existing models. Some AI jobs also train or fine-tune models. Those need more practice after this path.

You load data, call a model, check its answer, test it and watch how it behaves. Your web development skills carry over.

The best proof you can show an employer is an app you can explain, test and change. Framework names help you read job posts. Your projects show what you can do. Finishing this path does not guarantee a job.

**After this, you can** read an AI job post and spot what you already do.

**The skill that lasts:** matching your skills to what teams actually need.

## Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- What AI engineers do day to day.
- How AI engineering is different from training models.
- What you'll be able to build at the end of this path.

## Assignment

> **Why:** See what the job really is, which parts you already do, and set your capstone goal.
>
> **Don't skip:** You'll reread your goal and your reason when motivation dips.

1. Read the README of Alexey Grigorev's [AI Engineering Field Guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide). Write down the 5 tasks that show up most in job posts.
2. In your log, mark which of those tasks you've already done as a web developer and which are new to you.
3. Write your capstone goal in your log. This path ends with a Campus Assistant that answers students' repeated questions from approved sources. The author of this path builds it for IUB, inside the Events platform, for the website and the Android/iOS app. Write one sentence for yours: who will it help, and which repeated questions will it answer?
4. No platform of your own? Pick any community whose questions repeat, such as a club, a society or a team wiki. Most lessons work the same. The platform lessons have a smaller fallback: a simple web chat with two demo groups instead of universities, a phone-friendly web page instead of a native app, and a rehearsal pilot.
5. Spend 10 minutes writing in your log: why does this matter to you and to the people you care about? Be specific. Reread it when your motivation dips.

### Memory check · 10 min

6. Close this page. In your log, answer from memory, then check:
   - Which parts of an AI engineer's job are normal software engineering? ([where to check](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide))
   - How long is one study session, and how is it split? ([where to check](01-how-this-path-works.md))
7. If an answer is shaky, revisit just that section and try again. Finish your log with one result you checked and the exact file or small action to start next time. Two lines are enough.
