# What AI Engineers Do

*Section 1: Introduction · Lesson 2 of 41 · about 1.5 hours*

## Introduction

> **Most of this job is one you already do, plus a very confident new colleague.**

This path targets applied AI engineering: building useful software around existing models. Some AI roles also train or fine-tune models; those need additional practice after this path.

Good news: most of the work is normal software engineering. You load data, call a model, check its answer, test it and watch how it behaves. Your web development skills will help a lot.

Your strongest evidence will be an app you can explain, test and change when the requirements move. Framework names help you find a job listing; the project checks help you show what you can actually do. Completion alone is not a hiring guarantee.

**After this, you can** read an AI job post and spot what you already do.

**The skill that lasts:** matching your skills to what teams actually need.

## Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- What AI engineers do day to day.
- How AI engineering is different from training models.
- What you'll be able to build at the end of this path.

## Assignment

1. Read the README of Alexey Grigorev's [AI Engineering Field Guide](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide). Write down the 5 tasks that show up most in job posts.
2. In your log, mark which of those tasks you've already done as a web developer and which are new to you.
3. Write down your capstone goal. This path ends with a Campus Assistant that answers students' repeated questions from approved sources. This path's author builds it for IUB inside the Events platform, for the website and the Android/iOS app. No platform of your own? Choose any community whose questions repeat, such as a club, a society or a team wiki. The data, search, answer and evaluation lessons work the same. The platform lessons give a smaller fallback: a simple web chat with two demo groups instead of universities, a phone-friendly web page instead of a native app, and a rehearsal pilot.
4. Take 10 minutes and write in your log: why does this matter to you, and to the people you care about? Be specific. You'll reread it when motivation dips.

### Memory check · 10 min

5. Close this page. In your log, answer from memory, then check:
   - Which parts of an AI engineer's job are normal software engineering? ([where to check](https://github.com/alexeygrigorev/ai-engineering-field-guide))
   - How long is one study session, and how is it split? ([where to check](01-how-this-path-works.md))
6. If an answer is shaky, revisit just that section and try again. Finish your log with one result you checked and the exact file or small action to start next time. Two lines are enough.
