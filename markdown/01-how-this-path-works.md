# How This Path Will Work

*Section 1: Introduction · Lesson 1 of 42 · about 45 min*

## Introduction

*A club secretary asks if your Events platform could answer students' questions. You say yes, then find forty buzzwords online. This week cuts them down to one job description, one study routine and one promise: after any break, you only owe the next small step.*

> **One lesson, one checked result, one small next step. That is enough for today.**

Welcome! This path takes you from your first AI model call to a deployed app that you can test, debug and explain.

Each lesson points you to free material, then asks you to build something. Don't skip the building. That is where you learn.

**After this, you can** restart after any break with one small step.

**The skill that lasts:** running your own learning loop. You can reuse it for every new tool you meet.

## Lesson overview

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain where the path ends and how long it takes.
- Choose your study days and set a weekly target.
- Write a restart note and three rules for after a break.

## Assignment

> **Why:** Set up the routine and the log that carry you through 54 weeks, breaks included.
>
> **Don't skip:** Every lesson ends with a log entry, and the three rules are how you restart.

1. Read the [course page](README.md). It has 7 sections and 5 projects, plus optional Electives. The core path takes about 54 weeks and ends with your Campus Assistant.
2. Pick your study days and a fixed time, and add them to your calendar. Aim for about 9 hours a week: 6 sessions of 90 minutes, or fewer, longer ones. Set the same number as your weekly target on the [course page](README.md). It counts sessions per week, not a daily streak.
3. Create a private GitHub repository called `ai-learning-log` and add a file called `log.md`. This is where your notes go. Each project gets its own public repo later.
4. Plan each 90-minute session like this: about 30 minutes learning, 45 building, and 15 checking your work and writing a log entry. Take breaks when you need them. End every entry with two lines: one result you checked, and the exact file or 10-minute action to start with next time.
5. Add three rules to your log. After a break: “Check my last result, then take one small next step.” Low energy: “15 minutes of review counts. It doesn't have to finish a lesson.” Stuck: “After 20 minutes without a useful clue, make the problem smaller, check the docs, then ask with my attempt and the exact error.”
6. If two sessions in a row end at the same obstacle, go back only to the skill you're missing. Don't restart the course.
7. Mark exams, Ramadan, Eid and family events in your calendar now. Plan shorter sessions for those weeks. A longer break only moves your finish date.
